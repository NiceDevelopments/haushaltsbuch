How to budget and save on a low income

On a low income you don't save with percentages but with a small fixed amount that goes out automatically – say €30 by automatic transfer the day after payday. The money for it rarely comes from going without day to day; it comes from fixed costs that are too high and small spending you don't notice.

With about €14 a day for food, toiletries and everything else, the 50/30/20 rule is unrealistic. That's not failure, it's maths. So take a different approach:

Seven steps

  1. Start with a tiny fixed amount. €20 or €30 a month by automatic transfer. €30 is €360 a year – enough for broken glasses or an unexpected bill.
  2. Check fixed costs, not your coffee. Phone plan, energy, insurance, subscriptions: switching takes effort once and saves money every month. Finding subscriptions: cancel subscriptions you don't use.
  3. Check what you're entitled to. Housing support, child benefits or social tariffs for energy and transport often go unclaimed. Free debt or money advice services can help you check.
  4. Write everything down for four weeks. On a tight budget the small amounts – vending machine, bakery, delivery – are often the difference between ending the month in the black or in the red.
  5. Use a weekly grocery budget instead of a monthly one: how much to spend on groceries.
  6. Park one-off money straight away. Tax refund, bonus, cash gifts: whatever isn't planned for daily life goes straight to savings.
  7. Emergency fund first, everything else later. A small buffer keeps the next repair off your overdraft: emergency fund.

Questions

Is saving €20 a month even worth it?

Yes. It's less about interest than about broken glasses or a surprise bill not landing on your overdraft. €20 a month is €240 after a year.

What if I'm always short at month end?

First check fixed costs, then write down every expense for four weeks. If it still doesn't add up, a free debt or budgeting advice service is the right next step.

Should I pay in cash to spend less?

It helps some people because they see the money go. Just make sure cash spending gets written down too – otherwise it's missing at month end.
