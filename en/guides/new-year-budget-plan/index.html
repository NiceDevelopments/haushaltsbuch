New year budget plan: start fresh in 4 steps

Four steps are enough. Check what's in your account. Write down what goes out for sure. Work out what's left per day. Then, for 30 days, write down amounts only – nothing else.

Step 1: a money check-up after the holidays

After the holidays the account is often emptier than expected. Write down where things really stand: balance, unpaid bills, credit card statement, buy-now-pay-later instalments. Don't judge, just record. Next December, this is what you compare against.

Step 2: fixed and yearly costs

List everything that leaves every month for sure: rent, utilities, phone, insurance, subscriptions. A checklist: fixed expenses list.

January is a good month to deal with yearly costs, because many of them renew around now. Divide them by twelve and plan them in monthly: yearly expenses in a monthly budget.

While you're at it, cancel the subscriptions nobody uses: cancel subscriptions.

Step 3: one number per day

Daily budget = (income − fixed costs − savings) ÷ days in the month

This number is your compass for January. No savings amount yet? Use 0 for the first month. Work it out with the daily budget calculator.

Step 4: 30 days, amounts only

Many resolutions fail not for lack of willpower but because they take too much effort. So the first month has one rule: write down every expense with its amount, right away or the same evening. No categories, no goals, no guilt.

At the end of the month, look back once: what's left? Where did it pinch? Only then is it worth setting a savings amount or adding categories. More on sticking with it: expense tracking tips.

If you like a challenge, start with a no-spend challenge, but not as a permanent state.

With the app

Into the new year with Simple Household Budget

Enter your income and fixed costs once, yearly ones with their due date – the app deducts them in the month they're due. After that the home screen shows what's left this month, and adding an expense takes two taps: type the amount, save.

At the end of the month a Month end card appears under Analysis: if you like, type in your account balance. From the second month on, the app shows whether spending is missing that you didn't record. Once a year it asks: Are income and fixed costs still right?

Questions

Do I have to start on 1 January?

No. It's best to start on the day your pay arrives, so the budget month matches your money: budget from payday to payday.

What about the holiday spending?

It belongs to last year. The check-up shows what's still open, such as a credit card bill. Plan the outstanding amount into January as a fixed cost.

Paper or app?

Both work. What matters is having it to hand when you pay or in the evening. For paper: printable household budget.
