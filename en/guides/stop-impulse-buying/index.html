How to stop impulse buying: seven everyday tricks

What helps most against impulse buying is time between wanting and paying. With the 24-hour rule, anything unplanned goes on a wish list first. If it still matters the next day, you buy it. On top of that, remove triggers – no saved card details, no shop newsletters – and write down every expense right away.

Seven tricks

  1. The 24-hour rule. For unplanned buys above an amount you set (say €30), wait a day. For bigger amounts, a week.
  2. A wish list instead of a basket. Note what you want, with the date. Many items lose their appeal within a week without you buying anything.
  3. Count in hours of work. Divide the price by your hourly take-home pay (example below).
  4. Don't save card details. If you have to fetch your card for every online purchase, you buy less on a whim.
  5. Unsubscribe from shop newsletters and push notifications. Discount emails create needs that weren't there before.
  6. Shop with a list – and only go down the aisles you need.
  7. Write down every expense right away. Having to type the amount makes you notice it.

Your hourly take-home pay, roughly: monthly take-home pay divided by monthly working hours (about 173 at 40 hours a week). “Is this worth six hours of work?” sounds different from “Is €89 okay?”.

Already bought it?

Check the return period straight away. For online purchases in the EU you can usually withdraw within 14 days of delivery; elsewhere, and in shops, the retailer's policy applies.

Know your triggers

Impulse buys are rarely random. Common triggers are boredom, stress, rewarding yourself after a hard day or scrolling in the evening. If you know when you buy on impulse, you can push back right then – for example by moving shopping apps off your phone's home screen.

If buying feels out of control and debt is building up, debt advice and counselling services offer free help.

Questions

Is every spontaneous purchase a problem?

No. An ice cream on a hot day is fine. It's about purchases you regret soon after or that blow the monthly budget.

From what amount should the 24-hour rule apply?

Pick an amount that fits your budget – often €20 to €50. What matters is that it's set beforehand, not negotiated in the shop.

Does paying only in cash help?

For many people, yes, because they see the money go. That doesn't work online – there a wish list and unsaved card details help more.
