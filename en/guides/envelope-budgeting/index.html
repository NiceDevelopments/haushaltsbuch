Envelope budgeting: cash, accounts or digital

With envelope budgeting you split the money you have free each month into envelopes – one per spending area. When an envelope is empty, that area is done until next month. With real cash this is also called cash stuffing. Without cash you can do it with separate accounts or in a simpler form: a single digital “envelope” that shows what's left this month.

The principle in three steps

  1. Work out your spending money: income minus fixed costs minus savings. How: how much can I spend per day?
  2. Split it into envelopes – one for each area where you spend day to day.
  3. Only pay from the matching envelope. Empty is empty. Moving money between envelopes is allowed, but deliberately.

The buffer is the most important envelope. Without it the system collapses at the first flat bike tyre.

Many people start with six envelopes and end up with two or three after a few months: one for groceries, one for everything else – or just one.

How to start with cash

  1. Withdraw once, on payday – the whole month's spending money in one go.
  2. Ask for small notes, so you can pay exactly from each envelope.
  3. For online purchases, take the amount out of the envelope and pay it back into your account – otherwise online shopping escapes the system.

The simpler form: one envelope

If sorting is what makes you give up, one envelope for all everyday spending is enough. The limit is then not “fun is empty” but “the month is empty”.

For that to work you need a sense of pace: are you spending faster than the month is passing? The pace check is in how much can I spend per day?

Common mistakes

  • Budgeting too tightly. Envelopes with no slack lead to reshuffling after two weeks – and then to giving up.
  • Forgetting yearly bills. Insurance doesn't belong in the everyday envelope: yearly bills in a monthly budget.
  • Not counting card payments. If you pay both cash and card, both come out of the same envelope.

Questions

What happens to money left in an envelope at month end?

Decide in advance: roll it into next month, move it to savings, or collect it for something specific. More: money left at the end of the month.

How many envelopes make sense?

As few as possible. Three to six is a good start, and a buffer always belongs in there.

Can I move money between envelopes?

Yes, but openly: if you move money from “clothes” to “groceries”, you know there's less for clothes afterwards.
