How to budget with an irregular income

With an irregular income, don't plan with whatever just came in – pay yourself a fixed “salary”. All income goes into a buffer account first; from there you transfer the same amount to your current account every month. Set that amount between your weakest month and your average – and budget with it like a normal salary.

The principle: buffer and base salary

  1. Open a buffer account, for example an savings account you can withdraw from at any time. All income goes there.
  2. Set a base salary – the amount you transfer to yourself on the same day each month.
  3. Budget with the base salary as if it were a wage: subtract fixed costs, plan the rest.
  4. Surpluses stay in the buffer. If it grows bigger than needed, part can go to savings goals – or a pay rise for yourself.

If you're self-employed, set aside tax and pension contributions first. The base salary is what's left for personal spending.

How big the buffer should be

As a starting point: two to three base salaries before you start paying yourself a fixed amount. If your work is very seasonal, more. The buffer doesn't replace the emergency fund – that's for emergencies, the buffer for normal swings: emergency fund.

With the app

With Simple Household Budget

Enter your base salary as monthly income – on the day you transfer it to yourself. You can set the start of the budget to that exact day under Settings → Budget starts: budget from payday.

Income you want to spend right away can be added as income with the plus button in the history. If you change the base salary, the new amount applies from the next period. What stays in the buffer doesn't belong in the budget – otherwise the month looks bigger than it is.

Questions

What if the buffer runs dry?

Then the base salary is set too high. Lower it for the next few months and check your fixed costs until the buffer grows again.

Does this work for shift work or zero-hours contracts?

Yes. Wherever pay varies with hours, the same principle helps: plan with a cautious base amount and set aside anything above it for weaker months.

How often should I adjust the base salary?

About twice a year, using the last six to twelve months. Not after every good month.
