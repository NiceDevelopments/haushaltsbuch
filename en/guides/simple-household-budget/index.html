How to keep a simple household budget – no spreadsheet

A simple household budget has three parts: income, fixed costs and everyday spending. Subtract your fixed costs from your income once a month, then write down every expense – the amount is enough. Whatever is left is what you can still spend. You don't need a spreadsheet or categories.

The three numbers you need

  1. Income. What arrives each month after tax: your salary or wages, plus regular payments such as child benefit.
  2. Fixed costs. What goes out every month for sure: rent, utilities, insurance, phone, subscriptions. So you don't miss anything: fixed expenses list.
  3. Spending. Everything else – groceries, fuel, eating out, gifts. Each one on its own, as soon as it happens.

= Free for the month€820.00
− Spent so far€312.40

That last number is the whole point of a budget: it tells you every day how much is left.

The simplest version: amounts only

Most budgets don't fail because of the maths but because of the columns. If every coffee makes you decide between “Leisure” and “Food”, you'll soon stop.

The smallest version that still works: write down the amount and nothing else. No category, no description. To answer “How much is left?”, that's all you need.

Categories pay off once you have a specific question – say, how much do I spend on eating out? Then add that one category, not twelve at once.

How to keep it simple

  • Cash counts just like card. Small cash payments are exactly what's missing at the end of the month.
  • Don't aim for perfect. One forgotten coffee doesn't make the budget worthless. Comparing with your bank balance once a month is enough to spot gaps.
  • Take a quick look at month end, no long analysis: did it work out? What to do with what's left: money left at the end of the month.

With the app

How it works in Simple Household Budget

The app is built around this method. You enter income and fixed costs once during setup. After that, an expense means: type the amount, tap Save. The large number on the home screen is what's still available.

Category and description are optional. Hold receipts up to the camera instead of typing them in. No account, no bank connection – everything stays on your phone.

Questions

What is the simplest way to budget?

The one you'll actually stick with. For most people that means as few required fields as possible. The amount is enough; you deal with income and fixed costs once a month. Whether that's a notebook, a notes app or a budget app matters less.

Do I need categories?

No. To know how much is left, the amount is enough. Categories only help once you want to know where the money goes.

Isn't it enough to go through my bank statement once a month?

For looking back, yes. But then you only find out at the end of the month that money got tight. If you write down each expense right away, you know on the 15th how much is left.

Do I have to write down card payments too?

Yes – everything that isn't a fixed cost, whether paid in cash, by card or by phone. Otherwise the “still available” number is wrong.
